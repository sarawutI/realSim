    How rules work

    Each atom has an element (a–f, the colour) and a state (a number). Rules fire when two atoms touch:

    • e8 + e0 -> e4e3: two free atoms bond
    • x4y1 -> x2y5: two bonded atoms change state
    • f4f3 -> f8 + f8: a bond breaks

    x and y match any element; an x used twice means "the same element". : 0.4 at the end is the energy the reaction needs from the collision (negative = gives off heat). Energy is conserved; elements are never created or destroyed.

    What's real and what's simplified

    Real: conservation of matter and energy, thermal motion (Brownian/Langevin), reactions that need collision energy, limited food, natural selection with no fitness function.

    Built in for cells: membranes let free atoms through but not molecules, reseal when broken, and pinch apart between two genomes. What genes do (enzymes) is fixed; which genes survive is not.

    Simplified: 2D instead of 3D, six made-up elements, reactions given as rules rather than derived from quantum mechanics, a few thousand atoms instead of 10²³.
